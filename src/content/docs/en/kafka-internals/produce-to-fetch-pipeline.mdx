## The idea in one sentence

One `producer.send()` becomes a `ProduceRequest`, an append to a real segment file, a wait for the **high watermark** to advance (driven entirely by followers' own `FetchRequest`s, never a push from the leader), and only then does a `ProduceResponse` return -- a separate, independent `FetchRequest`/`FetchResponse` pair is what actually delivers the record to a consumer, bounded by that same high watermark.

<Callout title="What this lesson's broker can and can't show directly">
This lesson runs on this module's own single-broker KRaft container (replication factor 1 -- see [How every proof in this module was made](/kafka/en/kafka-internals/)). That is enough to prove every **client-facing** hop below with real request-log lines: `ProduceRequest`, the segment append, a consumer's `FetchRequest`/`FetchResponse`, and `OffsetCommit`. It is **not** enough to show a real leader→follower `FetchRequest` -- there is no follower on one broker. That hop is proven with real request-log evidence in [Replication and ISR Internals](/kafka/en/kafka-internals/replication-and-isr-internals/), which uses a temporary 3-broker cluster for exactly this reason. The Mermaid sequence below shows the full pipeline; the proofs under it are honest about which hops come from which lesson.
</Callout>

## The full sequence

<Mermaid title="One produce, one commit -- the real RPC sequence" code={pipelineDiagram} />

export const pipelineDiagram = `sequenceDiagram
  participant P as Producer
  participant L as Leader broker
  participant F as Follower broker
  participant C as Consumer
  P->>L: ProduceRequest (acks, records batch)
  L->>L: Append batch to active segment (.log)
  Note over L: baseOffset assigned, CRC written
  F->>L: FetchRequest (replicaId, fetchOffset)
  L->>F: FetchResponse (records, highWatermark)
  Note over L: HW advances once ISR has the offset
  L->>P: ProduceResponse (baseOffset) -- only after HW passes it, if acks=all
  C->>L: FetchRequest (fetchOffset, fetch.min.bytes, fetch.max.wait.ms)
  L->>C: FetchResponse (records bounded by HW)
  C->>L: OffsetCommit (group, committedOffset)`;

Six real hops, in order: (1) the producer sends a batch in one `ProduceRequest`; (2) the leader appends it to its active `.log` segment, assigning the batch's `baseOffset`; (3) a follower's *own* `FetchRequest` (not a leader push) is what replicates the batch; (4) once the ISR has the new offset, the leader advances the partition's **high watermark (HW)**; (5) with `acks=all`, the `ProduceResponse` only returns after that HW advance -- with `acks=1` it returns right after the local append, and with `acks=0` there is no response at all (proven below); (6) a consumer's independent `FetchRequest`/`FetchResponse` pair, bounded by the HW, is what actually delivers the record, followed by an `OffsetCommit`.

## Proof: a real `ProduceRequest` and the segment it appends to

Producing one record with `acks=all` and capturing this module's own broker's request log (`kafka.request.logger` at `TRACE` -- see the module index for how):

```text
$ /opt/kafka/bin/kafka-console-producer.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 \
    --topic internals-pipeline-demo --producer-property acks=all <<< "hello-acks-all"
```

Real, trimmed request-log line for the `PRODUCE` request this generated:


## Proof: `acks=0` gets no response at all, `acks=1` gets a real one

The three `acks` values are not just "more or less durable" -- they are three genuinely different **protocol** behaviors, provable directly from the request log. Producing the same topic with `acks=0` then `acks=1`, trimmed:

```text
--- acks=0 ---
"request":{"transactionalId":null,"acks":0,"timeoutMs":1500,"topicData":[...]},
"response":"",
"totalTimeMs":1.61, ...

--- acks=1 ---
"request":{"transactionalId":null,"acks":1,"timeoutMs":1500,"topicData":[...]},
"response":{"responses":[{"topicId":"...","partitionResponses":[
  {"index":0,"errorCode":0,"baseOffset":2,"logAppendTimeMs":-1,"logStartOffset":0,"recordErrors":[],"errorMessage":null}]}],"throttleTimeMs":0},
"totalTimeMs":0.989, ...
```

With `acks=0` the broker's own request log records `"response":""` -- there is no response body at all; the broker does the append and never replies. With `acks=1` the exact same append produces a real response carrying `baseOffset`. On this single-broker, RF=1 harness, `acks=1` and `acks=all` end up equivalent in durability (the ISR is just the leader), which is exactly why proving the *durability* difference between them needs a real ISR with more than one member -- that proof, with a real `min.insync.replicas` failure, lives in [Acks and Durability](/kafka/en/producers/acks-and-durability/) and [Replication and ISR Internals](/kafka/en/kafka-internals/replication-and-isr-internals/).

## Proof: a consumer's real `FetchRequest`/`FetchResponse`, bounded by the high watermark

Consuming the record back, request log for the resulting `FETCH`:

```text
[...] DEBUG Completed request:{"requestHeader":{"requestApiKey":1,"requestApiVersion":18,"correlationId":21,
"clientId":"console-consumer","requestApiKeyName":"FETCH"},
"request":{"maxWaitMs":500,"minBytes":1,"maxBytes":52428800,"isolationLevel":0,"sessionId":0,"sessionEpoch":0,
  "topics":[{"topicId":"FQwkK_oVSZeJP5dIDq-bSw","partitions":[
    {"partition":0,"currentLeaderEpoch":0,"fetchOffset":0,"lastFetchedEpoch":-1,"logStartOffset":-1,"partitionMaxBytes":1048576}]}],
  "forgottenTopicsData":[],"rackId":""},
"response":{"throttleTimeMs":0,"errorCode":0,"sessionId":125471525,"responses":[
  {"topicId":"FQwkK_oVSZeJP5dIDq-bSw","partitions":[
    {"partitionIndex":0,"errorCode":0,"highWatermark":1,"lastStableOffset":1,"logStartOffset":0,
     "abortedTransactions":null,"preferredReadReplica":-1,"recordsSizeInBytes":82}]}]},
"totalTimeMs":6.825,...} (kafka.request.logger)
```

The request names `fetchOffset:0` -- "give me records starting at offset 0." The response's `highWatermark:1` is the real proof of the bound: the broker will never hand a consumer a record past the HW, because a record past the HW hasn't been confirmed by the ISR yet and could, in principle, be lost on an unclean leader change (see [Replication and ISR](/kafka/en/topics-and-partitions/replication-and-isr/)). Then the commit:

```text
[...] DEBUG Completed request:{"requestHeader":{"requestApiKey":8,"requestApiVersion":9,"correlationId":23,
"clientId":"console-consumer","requestApiKeyName":"OFFSET_COMMIT"},
"request":{"groupId":"internals-pipeline-group","generationIdOrMemberEpoch":1,
  "memberId":"console-consumer-107950bc-df8e-47f2-85d7-5d428bfb0217","groupInstanceId":null,
  "topics":[{"name":"internals-pipeline-demo","partitions":[{"partitionIndex":0,"committedOffset":1,"committedLeaderEpoch":0,"committedMetadata":""}]}]},
"response":{"throttleTimeMs":0,"topics":[{"name":"internals-pipeline-demo","partitions":[{"partitionIndex":0,"errorCode":0}]}]},
"totalTimeMs":9.957,...} (kafka.request.logger)
```

`committedOffset:1` means "I have processed up through offset 0; resume at 1" -- the exact off-by-one semantics [Offsets and Commits](/kafka/en/consumers/offsets-and-commits/) already covers from the client side; this is the literal RPC that carries it.

## Proof: `fetch.max.wait.ms` is a real long-poll, not a guess

A `FetchRequest` with a high `fetch.min.bytes` and nothing new to return does not return instantly -- it holds the connection open up to `fetch.max.wait.ms`, a real server-side long poll:

```bash
# scripts/produce-to-fetch-pipeline/long-poll-timing.sh
TOPIC="${TOPIC_PREFIX}internals-longpoll-lesson"
/opt/kafka/bin/kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server $BOOTSTRAP \
  --create --if-not-exists --topic "$TOPIC" --partitions 1 --replication-factor 1

START=$(date +%s%3N)
/opt/kafka/bin/kafka-console-consumer.sh --bootstrap-server $BOOTSTRAP --topic "$TOPIC" \
  --group "${TOPIC_PREFIX}internals-longpoll-group" \
  --consumer-property fetch.min.bytes=1000000 --consumer-property fetch.max.wait.ms=3000 \
  --max-messages 1 --timeout-ms 6000 || true
END=$(date +%s%3N)
echo "elapsed_ms=$((END - START))"
```

Real output from this module's own broker (the same shape you will see against the harness broker, modulo container overhead): the fetch this generated logged `"maxWaitMs":3000,"minBytes":1000000` in the request and `"totalTimeMs":3004.636` in the response, with `"recordsSizeInBytes":0` -- the broker held the request open for essentially the full 3000ms before giving up and returning empty, because 1,000,000 bytes never accumulated on an otherwise-idle topic:

```text
"request":{"maxWaitMs":3000,"minBytes":1000000,...,"fetchOffset":3,...},
"response":{...,"highWatermark":3,"lastStableOffset":3,...,"recordsSizeInBytes":0}
"totalTimeMs":3004.636
```

This is the real mechanism behind "low `fetch.max.wait.ms` = more, smaller fetches; high `fetch.max.wait.ms` = fewer, larger fetches, at the cost of added latency when data is sparse" -- it is not a client-side sleep, it is the broker itself holding the request.

## Mental Model

A produce and a fetch are **two separate, independent RPC round trips that both key off the same two numbers on the leader: the log end offset (where new appends land) and the high watermark (how far a consumer, or `acks=all`, is allowed to see).** The leader never pushes data to a follower or a consumer -- both `FetchRequest`s are pulls, issued by the follower or the consumer on their own schedule (`fetch.max.wait.ms` bounding how long that pull can idle). `acks=all` is not a separate replication mechanism; it is the producer's `ProduceResponse` being held until the *same* HW-advance the followers' fetches already drive has passed the new record.

## Trade-offs

| Choice | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| `acks=0` | Lowest latency, no response wait at all (proven above: `"response":""`) | No delivery confirmation whatsoever -- a dropped request looks identical to a lost one |
| `acks=1` | A real response with `baseOffset` after one local append | Durable only as far as the leader; a leader crash before replication loses it |
| `acks=all` | Response waits for the ISR (durable across the whole ISR, not just the leader) | Higher latency; only as strong as the current ISR size (see [Replication and ISR Internals](/kafka/en/kafka-internals/replication-and-isr-internals/) for what happens when the ISR shrinks) |
| High `fetch.max.wait.ms` | Fewer, larger fetches -- less request overhead | Proven above: a consumer waits up to the full value when data is sparse |
| High `fetch.min.bytes` | Fewer round trips per byte transferred | Combined with a high `fetch.max.wait.ms`, can add real, provable latency (proven above: 3004ms) before an idle topic's data is delivered |

## Common Misconceptions

- **"The leader pushes new records to followers as soon as they're appended."** Proven above and in [Replication and ISR Internals](/kafka/en/kafka-internals/replication-and-isr-internals/): replication is follower-driven `FetchRequest`s, not a leader push.
- **"`acks=0` still returns a (fast) success response."** Proven above with the real request log: `acks=0`'s response field is literally `""` -- there is no response object, success or otherwise.
- **"A consumer can read a record the instant the leader appends it."** Proven above: the `FetchResponse`'s `highWatermark` bounds what a consumer can see; a record between the log end offset and the HW is invisible to consumers until the ISR catches up.
- **"`fetch.max.wait.ms` is a client-side polling interval."** Proven above: it is a parameter of the request itself (`"maxWaitMs":3000` inside the `FetchRequest`), and the broker's own logged `totalTimeMs` shows it actually held the connection, not the client sleeping and re-asking.

The comment's leap from "the response includes `baseOffset`" to "durably stored" is the bug, proven above: `acks=1`'s `ProduceResponse` (real request-log line above: `"baseOffset":2` with `acks:1`) only proves the **leader** appended the record -- it says nothing about the ISR. If this leader crashes before any follower's own `FetchRequest` has replicated that offset, the record is gone even though the producer already saw a successful response with a real `baseOffset`. For a payments topic, [Acks and Durability](/kafka/en/producers/acks-and-durability/) and [Replication and ISR Internals](/kafka/en/kafka-internals/replication-and-isr-internals/) are the two lessons that show the actual fix: `acks=all` plus `min.insync.replicas >= 2`, so a leader-only write either replicates before acking or fails visibly with `NotEnoughReplicasException` instead of silently risking loss.
</SpotTheBug>
